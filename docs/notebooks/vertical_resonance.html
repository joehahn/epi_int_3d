vertical_resonance: test particles at a satellite's vertical resonance (epi_int_3d)¶

A zero-mass ring, 81 circular streamlines of 6 particles spanning 1 ≤ r ≤ 1.04, under a vertical Fourier satellite (fast/satellite.py). Its m = 2 inner vertical resonance is at r_V = 1.02, mid-ring. The satellite (i_s = 0.01) grows as M_s(t) = 10⁻⁶(1 − e^{−t/τ}), τ = 10⁴, and the run lasts 2×10⁴ orbits (t = 1.26×10⁵, 12.6τ).

Expectations (notes/equations.md B6, B11; notes/phase2c/bending_wave_theory.md):

  1. Starting from z = v_z = 0, each particle's z(t) is the exact solution B11. Its vertical oscillation grows with the satellite's mass and settles into the forced response B6, z = F_z/(ν² − ω_m²), which changes sign across r_V.
  2. A free oscillation at ν remains, never decaying, of relative size ~1/(τ|ν − ω_m|). It is small except near r_V.
  3. The streamline at r_V grows without bound.
  4. Averaged over the last 10⁴ orbits, when the satellite is fully grown, each streamline's inclination is the forced inclination B12, i_f ≃ (M_s/M_p) i_s β_V² b_{3/2}^{(m)}(β_V)/[6(m−1)|x|] near r_V (Shu 1984's L_V/x).

Make the output with uv run python tools/run_simulations.py tests/vertical_resonance (about 30 s).

The simulation follows B11 to about 3×10⁻¹¹ in z everywhere. That is the leapfrog's O(dt²) error in the non-resonant part of the response, so relative to |z| it grows with |x|, up to 1.3×10⁻³ at the ring's edges. The streamline at r_V, where ν − ω_m ~ 10⁻¹⁴, agrees to 10⁻⁶. At the end, every streamline sits on the forced response B6, off by just B11's free part, which never decays.

Averaged over the last 10⁴ orbits, each streamline's inclination is the forced inclination to a median 3×10⁻⁴ (at most 2×10⁻³), even beside r_V, since the free part averages away (Fig. 2). The near-resonance form B12 is within 4% of the exact forced inclination out to |x| = 0.02.
