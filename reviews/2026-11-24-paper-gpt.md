### 1. Whole-paper assessment: simplification, physical explanations, and organization

- **Type:** other
- **Severity:** medium
- **Where:** `paper/epi_int_3d.tex`, Sections 2–4 and Appendices A–E; “This paper describes … a modest extension”
- **Finding:** The main physical argument is sound in outline: add a vertical oscillator to the drift, calculate gravity from the streamline’s shape, and test the resulting waves. It becomes harder to follow where an exactly invertible numerical map is described as exact orbital motion, where measured orbital elements are identified with displacement amplitudes, and where the steady and finite-time massless limits are combined.
- **Suggestion:** The following changes answer the three requested questions for the whole paper.

#### (1) Passages that could be simpler, shorter, or more correct, and (2) plain-language summaries of the derivations

Each entry gives a two-sentence physical summary, followed by the passage that would benefit from revision.

**a. Planetary potential and vertical frequency — Section 2.1, Equations (1)–(6).**

**Physics:** Near the equatorial plane, the planet’s potential is quadratic in height, so its vertical force is proportional to height. To first order in eccentricity and inclination, horizontal and vertical departures from a circular orbit evolve independently.

**Passage/problem/fix:** The sequence from Newton’s law through all three cylindrical equations is longer than needed for the new vertical result. Keep the potential, its vertical expansion, and the resulting frequency together; retain the angular-momentum equation where it motivates the coordinate-to-element map. Clarify that \(\nu(r)\) is a local restoring frequency, not a constant oscillator frequency along an eccentric orbit.

**b. Planar epicycle, frequency identity, and vertical elements — Section 2.2, Equations (7)–(12).**

**Physics:** The inherited planar map preserves the particle’s vertical angular momentum while approximating its radial epicycle to first order. Vertical motion adds an amplitude and phase, and the difference between orbital and vertical frequencies gives nodal regression.

**Passage/problem/fix:** The Laplace-equation identity interrupts the progression from the vertical equation to its solution, and Equation (9) repeats Equation (5) evaluated at \(a\). Put the frequency identity after the three frequencies have been introduced, eliminate the repeated display, and explicitly distinguish the implemented longitude-based phase from an exact harmonic-oscillator phase; the difference is \(O(ei)\).

**c. Inverse map and drift — Section 2.3 and Section 3.4, Equation (13).**

**Physics:** Angular momentum fixes the guiding radius, after which position and velocity determine the epicyclic amplitudes and phases. Advancing those phases and reversing the transformation gives a reversible map, although that alone does not make it an exact Hamiltonian flow.

**Passage/problem/fix:** “exact inverses” is clear and justified; “second-order symplectic” and “exact for the unperturbed motion” are stronger and obscure the distinction. Present the forward map, inverse map, and drift together, with one explicit statement of what is algebraically exact and what is only first-order physical accuracy.

**d. Three-dimensional wire gravity — Section 3.1 and Appendix A, Equations (14)–(16) and (A1)–(A8).**

**Physics:** A locally straight streamline pulls a particle toward the closest point on that line. Subtracting the separation’s component along the streamline gives the perpendicular separation and hence all three force components.

**Passage/problem/fix:** Appendix A derives this projection, then derives the near-point displacement again through several equivalent forms. Keep the crossing-point vector, tangent, projection, and component result as the main chain; retain the two-leg geometry only as needed for the figure and the density-evaluation argument.

**e. Planar limit and changes from the predecessor — Appendix B, Equations (B1)–(B2).**

**Physics:** In the ring plane, the wire force has a particularly simple radial component and a tangential component set by the wire’s slope. For a rotating wave, particle velocity is not tangent to the instantaneous streamline shape, so using velocity can give the wrong torque.

**Passage/problem/fix:** “Distance,” “Direction,” and “Linear density” mix exact geometry, implementation history, and unreported regression tests. Lead with the shape-versus-velocity correction, which matters physically for waves; shorten the distance correction and move the small regression-test numbers to a documented test summary.

**f. Free bending waves — Section 4.1.1, Equations (17)–(18).**

**Physics:** A corrugated ring’s self-gravity pulls it back toward a flat sheet, raising its vertical oscillation frequency. The dispersion relation determines where a wave can exist, and the outward-radiation condition selects its propagation direction and trailing sense.

**Passage/problem/fix:** The explanation is generally clear, but the assumptions behind the local sheet relation are not collected where it first appears. State the thin-sheet, linear, tightly wound approximation before using the relation, and distinguish the chosen resonant branch from the full satellite response.

**g. Satellite forcing and forced bending-wave solution — Section 4.1.2, Equations (19)–(29).**

**Physics:** An inclined satellite supplies periodic vertical forcing, whose Fourier components have different pattern speeds and resonances. Close to one resonance, self-gravity turns the resonant response into an outgoing wave plus a local forced oscillation.

**Passage/problem/fix:** The text makes the algebraic substitution \(k\rightarrow-i\,d/dr\) look more general than the one-sided wave approximation behind it. State that approximation, keep the differential equation and solution as required by P82, and qualify the later assertion that the entire solution tends to the massless forced response.

**h. Forced inclination of massless particles — Section 4.1.3, Equation (30).**

**Physics:** Away from resonance, a particle’s forced vertical displacement is the forcing divided by the frequency-squared detuning. Slow satellite growth reduces the accompanying free oscillation, but does not dissipate it.

**Passage/problem/fix:** The explanation becomes misleading at “what free motion remains averages away over time.” Explain the difference between averaging a signed or complex displacement and averaging the positive inclination, and identify any excluded near-resonance particles.

**i. Wavenumber and torque measurements — Section 4.1.4, Equation (31).**

**Physics:** Subtracting the local forced response leaves the propagating wave, whose radial phase gradient gives its wavenumber. The phase-correlated height also predicts the torque that a consistent satellite potential would exert.

**Passage/problem/fix:** The wavenumber explanation is good, but the torque discussion first describes an exerted torque and then says that the ring never feels it. Call the bending-wave quantity a reconstructed torque diagnostic from its first introduction, and separate that diagnostic from actual angular-momentum conservation.

**j. Density-wave counterpart — Sections 4.2.1–4.2.3, Equations (32)–(39).**

**Physics:** Radial self-gravity reinforces a radial density displacement rather than restoring it, reversing the sign of the self-gravity term relative to bending waves. That sign reversal sends an inner-Lindblad wave outward, while the same forced-plus-wave construction supplies its amplitude and torque.

**Passage/problem/fix:** The parallel presentation is appropriately brief, but repeatedly referring to bending-wave equations leaves substitutions implicit. Define the Lindblad version of \(\epsilon\) explicitly and state once that \(r_V,A_V\) become \(r_L,|\Psi|/r_L\); also correct the reference defining \(b_{1/2}^{(m)}\).

**k. Discrete gravity and pressure — Appendix C, Equations (C1)–(C4).**

**Physics:** A lattice of streamlines does not respond like a continuous sheet at wavelengths comparable to its spacing. Its central-difference pressure force misses the alternating-streamline mode entirely, while its gravity remains destabilizing.

**Passage/problem/fix:** This is one of the clearest derivations, but its direct connection to the wave-resolution errors is left unused. Add the vertical counterpart \(\omega_m^2=\nu^2+X(k\Delta)\), then use it to explain the bending-wave wavenumber and speed errors quantitatively.

**l. Viscous overstability — Appendix C, Equation (C5).**

**Physics:** Viscous stress can feed energy into an epicyclic disturbance rather than damp it when the stress response has the appropriate phase. Self-gravity lowers the oscillation frequency and makes this overstability easier to excite, while the discrete stencil modifies which wavelengths feel viscosity.

**Passage/problem/fix:** The transition from a first-order, uniform-lattice growth rate to a general resolution recommendation is too abrupt. State the weak-viscosity and local-uniformity restrictions, and separate the predicted radial overstability from the observed azimuthal interpolation instability.

**m. Ringlet mass–width scaling — Appendix C, paragraph beginning “That limit matters.”**

**Physics:** Self-gravity must couple a ringlet strongly enough to oppose differential precession caused by oblateness. Any resolution constraint then depends on how the required mass changes with width, eccentricity structure, and apsidal configuration.

**Passage/problem/fix:** The proposed dependence on apsidal twist is not derived, and the accompanying runs vary resolution rather than establishing the asserted width scaling. Label this an estimate and remove the universal “only” conclusion until a derivation and parameter scan support it.

**n. Continuum bending-wave restoring force — Appendix D, Equation (D1).**

**Physics:** Neighboring streamlines at different heights pull a displaced streamline toward the local sheet. Summing those pulls for a sinusoidal corrugation gives a restoring acceleration proportional to \(|k|Z\).

**Passage/problem/fix:** This derivation is already short, but the displayed integral uses the crest amplitude \(Z_0\) and then equates the result to an expression containing the local displacement \(Z\). State that the integral is evaluated at a crest, or carry the local \(Z(r)\) through the calculation.

**o. Massless limit — Appendix E, Equations (E1)–(E3).**

**Physics:** Outside the resonance, the steady solution approaches the forced particle response as self-gravity disappears. Inside the resonance, the steady wave amplitude instead grows without bound, whereas at fixed finite time its front travels an ever smaller distance.

**Passage/problem/fix:** The integration-by-parts derivation is clear; its opening and closing conclusions are not. State the noncommuting limits first, distinguish a finite-time massless response from a purely forced response, and identify the initialization or averaging needed to remove free motion.
